Hello! I'm a postdoctoral researcher at Princeton Neuroscience Institute. I’m interested in how people learn and make decisions. Human life is organised into sequences of discrete goals: cooking dinner, climbing mountains, answering emails. The demand for us to flexibly switch between these different tasks leads to a number of computational trade-offs on different timescales – such as deciding [when to abandon or persist](https://www.cell.com/trends/cognitive-sciences/fulltext/S1364-6613(25)00278-5) with tasks in the moment, and how to learn new information about these different tasks without interference. In my research I have investigated these trade-offs using a combination of computational modelling, neural imaging, and working with clinical populations including patients with prefrontal brain damage. 

I've been a C.V. Starr fellow with [Yael Niv](https://nivlab.princeton.edu/) at Princeton since September 2025. Before this, I did my PhD at the University of Oxford with [Chris Summerfield](https://humaninformationprocessing.com/), [Jill O'Reilly](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/people/jill-oreilly), and [Nils Kolling](https://sites.google.com/view/ecc-team/home). My previous research investigated the mechanisms that support persistence with [goals](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01844-5) and [planning](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/early/2025/01/29/JNEUROSCI.1814-24.2025). In more recent work, I looked at the question of why new learning sometimes interferes with prior knowledge and sometimes doesn't, in humans and artificial neural networks. In particular, I'm interested in the [trade-offs](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02318-y) between transferring knowledge to new settings, and avoiding forgetting.
